Welcome to the National Museum of Požarevac.

You are standing at the entrance to the main building, where the archaeological history of the Braničevo region is presented from prehistory to Late Antiquity.

Your visit begins with the Mesolithic, Neolithic, and Eneolithic periods, showcasing the emergence of settled communities, agriculture, cult practices, and early metallurgy. Pottery, tools, figurines, and other artifacts illustrate the lives of the region's earliest inhabitants.

The western section is dedicated to Viminacium, the largest city of Roman Upper Moesia. Exhibited objects, including jewelry, weapons, tools, glass and ceramic vessels, architectural fragments, and necropolis finds, reveal the city's economic, artistic, and spiritual life, as well as the high level of craftsmanship achieved between the 2nd and 4th centuries.

The eastern section presents the painted funerary architecture of Late Antiquity. Frescoes from the tombs of Viminacium demonstrate the coexistence of Christian beliefs and older pagan traditions, offering valuable insight into the visual culture of the period.

As the core of the museum complex, this building provides an overview of the Danube region's material culture, with special emphasis on prehistoric societies and the urban development of Viminacium.
